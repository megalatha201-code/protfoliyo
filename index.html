01
PROJECT CONTEXT

Overview

The Premise

Music streaming platforms house over 100 million songs, yet listeners consistently report feeling stuck in repetitive recommendation loops. When users want music to support an immediate context—whether late-night studying, unwinding after work, or a morning run—they are forced to sift through endless curated playlists or type vague search queries that fail to deliver.

Spotify AI Mood re-architects music exploration by introducing contextual mood prediction directly into the Spotify interface, balancing intelligent recommendations with complete user control.

Design Scope & Deliverables

  • Empathetic User Research: Qualitative interviews and pain point synthesis.
  • Competitor Benchmarking: Analyzing 4 direct platforms and 1 indirect app.
  • Information Architecture & User Flow: Frictionless branching for new and returning users.
  • Lo-Fi to Hi-Fi Prototyping: Hand-drawn wireframes translated into Spotify Dark UI.
  • Design System Tokens: Cohesive color tokens, typography scales, and accessible contrast.
02
THE CORE FRICTION

Problem Statement

⚠️
“Users spend too much time searching for music that matches their current mood, while existing recommendations can sometimes feel repetitive or irrelevant.”

Identified Research Pain Points

PAIN POINT 01

Repetitive Recommendations

Algorithms repeatedly recommend the same tracks from the user’s past history rather than helping them discover fresh, context-fitting music.

PAIN POINT 02

Difficult Music Discovery

Discovering new artists or genres that fit a specific mindset requires manual digging through dozens of generic editorial playlists.

PAIN POINT 03

Home Screen Feels Crowded

The standard Spotify home feed is overloaded with podcasts, audiobooks, and mixed banners, causing visual fatigue and decision paralysis.

PAIN POINT 04

Finding Playlists Takes Time

Listeners spend 5 to 15 minutes skipping songs and scrolling playlists just to find the right background track for work or relaxation.

PAIN POINT 05

Search Doesn't Match User Intent

Keywords like "calm coding" or "energized morning" yield static playlists with mixed lyrical songs that frequently disrupt focus.

PAIN POINT 06

Users Want Better Personalization

Listeners crave recommendations tailored to their actual time of day, activity, and energy levels without complex manual configuration.

😊 Satisfied Listener

Key Research Notes & Findings

  • Context Trumps Genre: 85% of participants listen to music based on their immediate task (coding, commute, winding down) rather than a strict genre (rock, classical).
  • The 3-Skip Threshold: If the first 3 recommended tracks do not fit the mood, users close the app or switch to another platform.
  • Time Sensitivity: Morning hours require low-energy calm or rising tempo, while late evening requires chill instrumental ambience.
  • User Agency is Crucial: Users do not want a "black box" algorithm; they want to easily tweak the mood if the prediction is off.
